 I built a webpage containing three aligned pricing cards and a centered login form using Flexbox properties. For the pricing cards, I created Basic, Pro, and Premium plans, each with a price, a features list showing projects, storage, support and analytics, and a choose button. I applied display flex on the parent pricing-container and used flex-direction row to keep cards horizontally aligned, justify-content center to horizontally center the entire group of cards in the middle of the page, and align-items stretch to make all cards equal in height even though their content differs. Inside each card I also used flexbox with flex-direction column and justify-content space-between to stack content vertically and push the button to the bottom. For the login form section, I demonstrated perfect centering by making the login-section container display flex with justify-content center for horizontal centering and align-items center for vertical centering along with min-height 400px to allow vertical centering to be visible, and inside the form I used flex-direction column to arrange email, password and login button vertically with align-items center to keep them centered. I also demonstrated practical use of flex-direction by showing two examples: one with row where items flow left to right, and one with column where items flow top to bottom, proving that flex-direction controls the main axis of the flex container. This assignment shows how Flexbox simplifies both card layouts and perfect centering which was very difficult before Flexbox.
